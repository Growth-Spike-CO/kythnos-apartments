Things to Do in Kythnos

Home / Guides / Things to Do in Kythnos

Kythnos rewards visitors who want more than beaches. The island's two main villages preserve traditional Cycladic life, while ancient ruins and thermal springs connect you to thousands of years of history. You can walk through a former iron mine, soak in warm mineral water by the sea, or follow paths to sanctuaries where people worshipped in the 10th century BC.

Explore the Villages

Chora (Messaria)

The main town sits inland, its narrow streets lined with whitewashed houses and blue-domed churches. The Archaeological Museum reopened in 2023 and displays finds from Vryokastro, oria Kastro, and Maroula, giving context to the island's long occupation. Walk through the quiet alleys in the evening when locals gather in the small squares.

Dryopida

About 300 people live in this traditional village, the second-largest settlement on Kythnos. Red-tiled roofs distinguish it from other Cycladic towns, and the houses cluster on a hillside in a way that feels untouched by tourism. The village serves as the gateway to Katafyki Cave.

Visit Natural and Historical Sites

Katafyki Cave

One of the biggest caves in Greece, katafyki operated as an iron mine before opening to visitors. A free guided tour takes about 15 minutes and leads you through chambers where miners once worked. The cave stays cool even in summer, and the rock formations create strange shapes in the dim light.

Loutra Thermal Springs

Mineral-rich warm water flows directly into the sea at Loutra, where people have bathed since antiquity. The water temperature stays constant year-round, and you can soak where the thermal springs meet the waves. Small guesthouses and tavernas cluster around the springs, making it easy to spend a full day here.

Vryokastro

The ancient capital of Kythnos was occupied from the 10th century BC to the 7th century AD. Sanctuaries dedicated to Apollo, artemis, Aphrodite, and Demeter still mark the site, though most structures exist as foundations and scattered stones. A walking path from Chora brings you to the ruins, which overlook the sea on a windswept plateau.

Hike and Eat

Hiking Trails

Trails cross the island, connecting villages, beaches, and archaeological sites. The paths follow old routes that locals used before roads, passing through valleys and along ridgelines with views of neighboring islands. Spring brings wildflowers, while autumn offers cooler temperatures for longer walks.

Local Tavernas

Small tavernas in the villages serve Cycladic food made from what grows on the island or comes from nearby waters. Expect grilled fish, tomato fritters, local cheeses, and vegetables prepared simply. The portions are generous, and the owners often know the fishermen or farmers who supplied the ingredients.
