Welcome to Kythnos

Kythnos sits in the Western Cyclades, close enough to Athens for a weekend escape but far enough to feel genuinely Greek. You won't find the crowds that pack Mykonos or Santorini. Instead, you'll discover an island where locals still outnumber tourists, where traditional villages cling to hillsides, and where more than 70 beaches wait to be explored.

The island goes by two names. Locals often call it Thermia, a nod to the thermal springs that have drawn visitors since ancient times. These hot springs still bubble up at Loutra, where you can soak in mineral-rich waters that flow directly into the sea.

Three main settlements anchor island life. Merichas, the port on the west coast, serves as the arrival point and has grown into a lively hub with tavernas and cafes along the waterfront. Chora, also known as Messaria, spreads across an inland hillside as the island's capital. Dryopida, the most traditional of the three, preserves centuries-old architecture with its red-tiled roofs and narrow lanes.

Kolona beach has become the island's most photographed spot: a thin strip of sand connecting Kythnos to a small islet, with turquoise water on both sides. But dozens of other beaches remain quiet even in August, many accessible only by boat or unpaved road.

How to Get to Kythnos

Ferries from Lavrio

Lavrio offers the quickest route to Kythnos. The port sits on the eastern coast of Attica, about an hour's drive from Athens center and closer to the airport than Piraeus.

The fastest ferries complete the crossing in around 1 hour and 10 minutes. Most services take about 1 hour and 37 minutes. From June through September, roughly 19 weekly crossings operate on this route. Several ferry companies run the service: Blue Star Ferries, goutos Lines, triton Ferries, and Karistia.

All ferries dock at Merichas, the island's main port on the west coast.

Ferries from Piraeus

Piraeus provides an alternative departure point, particularly if you're staying in central Athens or arriving without a car. The larger port offers more frequent connections to the broader Cyclades network.

Conventional ferries from Piraeus take about 3 hours to reach Kythnos. High-speed services, operated by Zante Ferries, cut the journey to around 1 hour.

The choice between Lavrio and Piraeus often comes down to your starting point in Athens and your schedule. Lavrio wins for speed and convenience if you're driving. Piraeus works better if you're relying on public transport from the city center.

Getting Around Kythnos

Local Buses

Two bus routes connect the main settlements. One line runs between Merichas, messaria (Chora), and Loutra. The other links Merichas with Dryopida and continues to Kanala.

These buses serve residents as much as visitors, so schedules align with local needs rather than tourist convenience. Services are limited, particularly outside summer months.

Taxis and Water Taxis

A small fleet of taxis operates on the island. You'll find them at the port when ferries arrive, and your accommodation can usually arrange a pickup. Book ahead during August when demand peaks.

Water taxis run from Merichas to various beaches around the coast. This option works well for reaching swimming spots without road access. Negotiate the fare before departure and confirm the pickup time for your return.

Rental Cars and Scooters

Renting your own wheels gives you the most freedom to explore. Several agencies operate in Merichas, and many can deliver vehicles to your accommodation.

The main roads between villages are paved and well-maintained. However, many beach access roads remain unpaved. A regular car handles most routes without trouble, but ground clearance helps on rougher tracks. Scooters work well for the main roads but struggle on loose gravel.

Fuel stations are limited. Fill up in Merichas or Chora before heading to remote areas.

Traffic is light year-round. The biggest challenge comes from narrow village streets and the occasional goat blocking a mountain road. Drive slowly, stay alert for oncoming vehicles on blind curves, and you'll manage fine.
