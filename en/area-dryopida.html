A Traditional Village in Kythnos

Dryopida sits in a green valley at the centre of Kythnos, away from the coast and the ferry crowds. This is the island's second-largest settlement, and it feels entirely different from the harbour villages. Red-tiled roofs cascade down the hillside, narrow lanes wind between whitewashed houses, and the pace of life slows right down.

The village has kept its authentic character. You'll find a few tavernas serving locals rather than tour groups, small shops that open when they open, and elderly residents sitting outside their homes in the late afternoon. It's quiet here, especially outside summer, and that's exactly the appeal.

Katafyki Cave

Dryopida's main attraction is Katafyki Cave, one of the largest caves in the Cyclades. The cave system extends deep into the hillside, with chambers that were once used for iron ore mining. You can walk through sections of the cave and see the old mining infrastructure still in place.

The temperature inside stays cool year-round, which made it a natural refuge during summer heat and times of danger. Local guides sometimes offer tours that explain the geology and history, though you can also explore the accessible areas on your own.

Getting to Dryopida

The village sits about 10 minutes by car from Merichas port, connected by a winding road that climbs inland. You'll also find it's roughly the same distance from the west-coast beaches like Episkopi and Apokrousi.

Most visitors rent a car or scooter to reach Dryopida, as bus services to the interior villages run less frequently than coastal routes. The drive itself is pleasant, passing through the island's greener central landscape.

Who Should Stay in Dryopida

This village suits people who want to experience traditional island life rather than beach-resort convenience. If you're happy with a quieter base and don't mind a short drive to reach the coast, staying in this area offers a different perspective on Kythnos.

It works well for couples seeking peace, for visitors interested in walking and exploring inland trails, and for anyone who prefers village rhythms to waterfront activity. You'll need your own transport, but you gain proximity to both coasts and a genuine sense of place.
