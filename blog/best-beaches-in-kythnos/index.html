The Best Beaches in Kythnos: A Guide to the Island's Finest Shores

Written by Vrettos Goumas · 2026-05-03

Home / Journal / The Best Beaches in Kythnos: A Guide to the Island's Finest Shores

Kythnos hides around 70 beaches along its coastline, from organized strips with sunbeds to wild coves you reach on foot through scrubland. The island's beaches divide naturally by coastline, each side offering different water conditions and atmospheres. Here are the ones worth planning your day around.

Northwest Coast: Organized Beaches and Thermal Springs

The northwest side catches less wind and hosts the island's most developed beaches. Episkopi stretches wide with clear shallow water, making it popular with families. You'll find sunbeds, umbrellas, and a beach bar here. The sand is coarse but clean, and the water stays calm most days.

Loutra sits at the northern tip where thermal springs bubble up and meet the sea. The water here runs warmer than other beaches, especially near the rocks where the springs emerge. It's a small beach, partly organized, with a few tavernas within walking distance. The combination of warm mineral water and regular seawater creates an unusual swimming experience.

Apokrousi ranks among the largest beaches on Kythnos. The water here glows a particular shade of blue that photographs well but looks even better in person. The beach has some organization but plenty of space to spread out beyond the sunbed rows. It sits near Episkopi, reachable by the same coastal road.

The Kolona Peninsula: Kythnos at Its Most Photogenic

Kolona is the beach that appears in every Kythnos photograph. A narrow sandbar connects the main island to the small islet of Agios Loukas, creating two beaches that face opposite directions. You can swim in the sea on your left, walk ten meters across sand, and swim in the sea on your right. The water stays shallow for a long stretch, and the sand is fine and pale.

The beach has no facilities, so bring water and shade. A dirt road leads here from the main road, passable by car but rough in places. Most people arrive by mid-morning and stay until the light shifts in late afternoon.

Fykiada lies a short walk south from Kolona, though most visitors arrive by boat. The beach curves in a protected bay with golden sand and no development. The walk from Kolona takes about 20 minutes over rocky terrain, so wear proper shoes if you're hiking. The isolation means you often share the beach with just a handful of others.

East Coast: Family Beaches and Quiet Coves

Kanala and Megali Ammos sit on the eastern shore, sheltered from the northwestern winds that can whip up the other side. Kanala is the more organized of the two, with a few rooms to rent and a taverna nearby. Megali Ammos, as the name suggests, is the larger beach, with soft sand and gentle water that works well for children. Both beaches see consistent sun throughout the day.

The east side generally offers calmer conditions when the rest of the island deals with wind. The water here tends toward turquoise rather than the deeper blue of the western beaches.

Small Beaches Worth the Detour

Martinakia sits close to Merichas port, making it an easy option if you arrive late or leave early. It's small and pebbly but the water is clean and it rarely gets crowded. A few trees provide natural shade.

Naoussa, sometimes called Aosa, is the kind of beach you find by asking locals. It's small and picturesque, tucked into a cove with clear water. Access requires either a boat or a willingness to navigate unmarked paths.

Agios Stefanos, lefkes, and Flampouria round out the options. Each has its adherents among regular visitors, people who return to the same beach every summer because they know exactly where the afternoon shade falls or which rocks shelter the best snorkeling.

Practical Notes

Most organized beaches charge around 8 to 10 euros for two sunbeds and an umbrella. Wild beaches cost nothing but offer nothing. Bring your own water, snacks, and something for shade. A rental car or ATV makes reaching multiple beaches in one day possible, though some of the most beautiful spots require a final approach on foot. The caique boats that run between beaches in summer offer an alternative for those staying near the ports.

The beaches here don't come with beach clubs or loud music. What they offer instead is clean water, reliable sun, and enough variety that you can match the beach to your mood each day.
