The Best Beaches in Kythnos: Our Island Guide

Written by Vrettos Gkoumas · 2026-05-03

Kythnos has around seventy beaches. That number surprises people. Most visitors stick to three or four, which is fine, but I always tell our guests that half the fun is exploring. Some beaches you drive to, some you walk to, a few you reach only by boat. I'll take you through the ones we send people to most often, grouped by which side of the island they're on.

The Northwest Side

This is where most of the organized beaches sit. If you want a sunbed and a cold drink brought to you, start here.

Episkopi

Episkopi is the main organized beach on the northwest coast. Sunbeds, umbrellas, a couple of tavernas behind the sand. The water is clear and the beach is wide enough that it never feels too crowded, even in August. We send families here because the sea stays shallow for a good distance out. It's a five-minute drive from Merichas port.

Apokrousi

One of the largest beaches on Kythnos. The water is that particular shade of blue that photographs never quite capture. Apokrousi sits near Episkopi, so you can visit both in one morning. It's less organized than Episkopi but still easy to reach by car. I prefer it when I want space to spread out.

Martinakia

Small, close to Merichas. A quick stop if you're staying near the port and want to swim before dinner. Nothing fancy, just a small cove with clear water. Ten minutes on foot from the harbour.

The Iconic Double Beach

Kolona

This is the beach everyone photographs. A narrow sandbar connects the main island to the Agios Loukas islet, with sea on both sides. You can swim on the left, walk ten steps, swim on the right. The sand is pale and fine. Kolona gets busy in summer because it's so distinctive, but it's worth the company. You reach it by car on a dirt road, and the last stretch is rough. Go slowly. Some people anchor boats just offshore and swim in.

Fykiada

Near Kolona but wilder. Golden sand, no facilities, no shade. You walk to Fykiada from Kolona or come by boat. We tell guests to bring water and an umbrella if they plan to stay. The isolation is the point. On a calm day it's one of the most beautiful spots on the island.

The East Side

Kanala and Megali Ammos

Kanala sits on the east coast near the church of Panagia Kanala. The beach here, megali Ammos, is family friendly. Shallow, calm, organized. There's a taverna and the church is worth visiting. The icon inside is famous, and the view from the courtyard looks straight down the coast. We always suggest Kanala for anyone travelling with small children.

The Thermal Coast

Loutra

Loutra is not just a beach. Thermal springs flow into the sea here, so the water is warm in patches. People come for the therapeutic properties. The village of Loutra is tiny and quiet, with a few rooms to rent and a couple of tavernas. The beach itself is pebbly and the water is calm. If you like the idea of a natural hot bath by the sea, this is your spot.

The Smaller Coves

Kythnos hides dozens of small beaches between the famous ones. Naoussa, sometimes called Aosa, is picturesque and small. Agios Stefanos and Lefkes are quiet, less visited. Flampouria is another small cove. These beaches don't have facilities. You bring what you need and you leave no trace. That's the trade. Solitude for self-sufficiency.

How We Choose

I always ask guests what kind of day they want. Organized and easy? Episkopi or Kanala. Dramatic and iconic? Kolona. Quiet and wild? Fykiada or one of the smaller coves. Therapeutic? Loutra, no question.

The dirt roads to some beaches are rough. A small car is fine, but take it slow. Bring water, especially to the wild beaches. Bring shade if you burn easily. The summer sun here is serious.

Most of all, visit more than one. The variety is the gift. Kythnos gives you seventy beaches. Use them.
