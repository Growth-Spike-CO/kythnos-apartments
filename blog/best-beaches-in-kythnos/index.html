The Best Beaches in Kythnos: A Guide to the Island's Shores

2026-05-03

Home / Journal / The Best Beaches in Kythnos: A Guide to the Island's Shores

Kythnos has around 70 beaches scattered across its coastline, ranging from the famous double-sided sandbar at Kolona to hidden coves you can only reach on foot. The island's beaches fall naturally into groups based on which side of Kythnos they occupy, making it easier to plan your days by the sea.

Northwest Coast: Organized Beaches and Thermal Waters

The northwest side of Kythnos offers some of the island's most accessible beaches, including options with sunbeds and tavernas nearby.

Episkopi

Episkopi is one of the organized beaches on the northwest coast. You'll find clear water here and the convenience of facilities if you want to spend a full day by the sea without packing supplies. The beach sits close enough to the main road that getting there takes little effort.

Apokrousi

Near Episkopi, apokrousi ranks among the largest beaches on Kythnos. The water here shows that distinctive clear blue you see in photos of the Cyclades. The size of the beach means you can usually find space even during busier periods, and the proximity to Episkopi makes it easy to visit both in one day.

Loutra

Loutra offers something different: thermal springs that flow directly into the sea. The warm water mixing with the cool Aegean creates an unusual swimming experience. The small settlement at Loutra has been known for these springs since ancient times, and today you can combine a beach visit with a soak in the thermal waters.

The Icon: Kolona and Nearby Fykiada

Kolona

Kolona is the beach that appears in nearly every photo of Kythnos. A narrow sandbar connects the main island to the small islet of Agios Loukas, creating two beaches in one. You can swim on either side of the sandbar, with the sea lapping at both edges. The shallow turquoise water and the unique geography make this the most photographed spot on the island. Getting to Kolona requires either a boat trip or a walk from the nearest road access point.

Fykiada

Near Kolona, fykiada offers golden sand and a wilder setting. This beach sees fewer visitors because you need to reach it either on foot or by boat. The lack of facilities and the effort required to get there mean you'll often have long stretches of sand to yourself. Pack water and snacks if you're planning to stay for several hours.

East Coast: Family Beaches

Kanala and Megali Ammos

The east side of Kythnos has beaches that work well for families. Kanala, with its nearby beach of Megali Ammos, offers calmer conditions than some of the more exposed western shores. The area has a relaxed atmosphere, and the beaches here tend to have gentler entry into the water, which matters if you're bringing children. Kanala also has a small settlement nearby, so you're not completely isolated if you need supplies.

Near Merichas: Quick Access from the Port

Martinakia

Martinakia sits close to Merichas, the main port of Kythnos. This small beach offers convenience if you're staying near the port or if you want a quick swim without traveling far. The proximity to Merichas means it can get busier than more remote options, but it also means you can walk there easily.

Smaller Beaches Worth Finding

Beyond the main beaches, kythnos has several smaller options that reward the effort of finding them.

Naoussa (Aosa)

Naoussa, also called Aosa, is a small, picturesque beach that captures the quiet side of Kythnos. The beach remains relatively uncommercialized, offering a simple day by the sea without the infrastructure of the organized beaches.

Agios Stefanos, lefkes, and Flampouria

These three beaches represent the variety you'll find across Kythnos. Each has its own character, from the setting to the type of shore. Visiting several smaller beaches lets you discover which style of beach suits you best, whether you prefer sand or pebbles, shelter or exposure to the wind, crowds or solitude.

Planning Your Beach Days

The beaches of Kythnos divide roughly into organized options with facilities and wild beaches where you'll need to bring everything. The organized beaches cluster mainly on the northwest coast, while the more remote options require either a hike or a boat trip. With 70 beaches total, you could spend weeks here and still find new coves to explore. Start with the accessible beaches to get your bearings, then venture to the wilder shores once you understand the island's layout.
