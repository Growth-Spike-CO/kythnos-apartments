How to Get to Kythnos

2026-04-12

Home / Journal / How to Get to Kythnos

Getting to the Ports from Athens

Kythnos sits in the Western Cyclades, close enough to Athens that you can leave the airport in the morning and be swimming in the Aegean by early afternoon. Most visitors arrive through one of two ports: Lavrio or Piraeus.

Lavrio is the closer option if you're coming from Athens International Airport. The drive takes about an hour along a coastal road that skirts the eastern edge of Attica. KTEL buses run this route regularly, connecting the airport directly to the port. If you're traveling with luggage or prefer flexibility, a taxi or pre-booked transfer makes the journey straightforward. The port itself is compact and easy to navigate, with ticket offices and cafes clustered near the departure gates.

Piraeus is the larger alternative, located southwest of Athens city center. This port handles more ferry traffic and offers connections to islands across the Aegean. Getting there from the airport takes longer, usually around 90 minutes depending on traffic. The metro provides a direct link from the airport to Piraeus, though you'll need to change trains at one of the central stations. Buses also run this route, and taxis are readily available if you're hauling beach gear and suitcases.

Ferry Crossings to Merichas

All ferries to Kythnos dock at Merichas, the island's main port on the west coast. The village spreads along a wide bay with a sandy beach, tavernas, and accommodation within walking distance of the harbor.

From Lavrio

The Lavrio route is the fastest way to reach Kythnos. The quickest crossings take about 1 hour and 10 minutes, while most trips average around 1 hour and 37 minutes. Between June and September, roughly 19 weekly sailings operate on this route, run by Blue Star Ferries, goutos Lines, triton Ferries, and Karistia. The frequency drops outside peak season, so check current schedules if you're traveling in spring or autumn.

These ferries vary in size and speed. Some are conventional car ferries with indoor seating, outdoor decks, and a small cafe. Others are faster vessels that skip intermediate stops. Book tickets in advance during July and August when Greek families and European visitors fill the boats.

From Piraeus

Piraeus offers two types of service. Conventional ferries take about 3 hours to reach Merichas, stopping at other islands along the way. These larger ships have multiple decks, restaurants, and airplane-style seating. High-speed ferries operated by Zante Ferries cut the journey to about 1 hour, similar to the Lavrio crossing. The trade-off is price: faster boats cost more, and they're more affected by rough weather.

The Piraeus route makes sense if you're staying in central Athens before your trip or if the Lavrio schedule doesn't match your arrival time. The port is well-connected to the city, and the longer crossing gives you time to settle in, grab a coffee, and watch the islands emerge from the haze as you head south.

Getting Around Kythnos

Once you step off the ferry in Merichas, you have several options for exploring the island. Kythnos is small enough that you can reach most villages within 20 minutes by car, but public transport runs on a limited schedule.

Local Buses

Two bus routes serve the island. One connects Merichas with Messaria (the main town, also called Chora) and continues north to Loutra, a coastal village known for its thermal springs. The other route runs from Merichas south to Dryopida, a hillside settlement with red-tiled roofs, and ends at Kanala, where a small church sits on a rocky promontory above the sea.

Buses coordinate with ferry arrivals during summer, but services are infrequent. If you're planning to rely on public transport, ask your accommodation for the current timetable or check at the bus stop near the port.

Taxis and Water Taxis

Taxis wait at the port when ferries dock. The island has a small fleet, so you might need to call ahead if you're traveling outside peak hours. Water taxis operate from Merichas to some of the beaches that lack road access, offering a scenic alternative to driving.

Rental Vehicles

Renting a car or scooter gives you the freedom to explore at your own pace. Several agencies operate near the port, and many offer delivery to your accommodation. The main roads connecting the villages are paved and well-maintained, but some routes to remote beaches remain unpaved. A small car handles these tracks without trouble, though you'll want to take it slow on the rougher sections.

Scooters are popular with solo travelers and couples. The distances are short, the hills are manageable, and you can park almost anywhere. Just remember that some of the unpaved roads get dusty in summer and slippery after rain.
