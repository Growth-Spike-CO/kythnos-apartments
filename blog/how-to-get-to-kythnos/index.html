How to Get to Kythnos

Written by Giannis Goumas · 2026-04-12

Home / Journal / How to Get to Kythnos

Reaching Kythnos from Athens

Kythnos sits in the Western Cyclades, close enough to Athens to make it an easy escape from the capital. The island's main ferry port is Merichas, on the west coast, and you have two mainland ports to choose from: Lavrio and Piraeus. Each offers different advantages depending on your schedule and where you're starting from.

From Lavrio: The Faster Route

Lavrio gives you the quickest crossing to Kythnos. The fastest ferries make the journey in about 1 hour and 10 minutes, though most crossings average around 1 hour and 37 minutes. Between June and September, you'll find roughly 19 weekly departures, operated by Blue Star, goutos, Triton, and Karistia. This frequency means you can plan your arrival with flexibility.

Getting to Lavrio from Athens airport takes about an hour. You can drive if you have a rental car, or take the KTEL bus service that connects the airport to the port. The bus runs regularly and drops you within walking distance of the ferry terminal. If you're staying in central Athens before your trip, factor in the journey time to Lavrio when booking your ferry.

Why Choose Lavrio

The shorter crossing time makes Lavrio appealing if you're prone to seasickness or traveling with young children. The port itself is smaller and less hectic than Piraeus, which can make the boarding process more relaxed. If your flight lands at Athens airport in the morning, you can often catch an afternoon ferry without needing to spend a night in the city.

From Piraeus: More Frequency, longer Journey

Piraeus, greece's largest port, offers conventional ferries that take about 3 hours to reach Kythnos. High-speed services operated by Zante Ferries cut this down to roughly 1 hour. The conventional ferries cost less and give you time to settle into island mode, watch the sea, and maybe grab a coffee on deck.

Piraeus connects to Athens by metro, making it straightforward if you're staying in the city center. The metro ride takes about 30 to 40 minutes from Syntagma Square, and trains run frequently throughout the day. From the airport, you can take the metro with one change, or opt for a direct bus or taxi.

Conventional or High-Speed

The 3-hour conventional crossing gives you a proper transition from city to island. You'll likely stop at one or two other Cycladic islands along the way, and the slower pace means calmer seas feel more comfortable. High-speed ferries get you there faster but can feel bumpier in rough weather. Check the forecast and your own tolerance for motion before deciding.

Booking Your Ferry

Ferry schedules change with the seasons. Summer brings more frequent departures, while winter sees reduced services. Book tickets online a few days ahead during peak season, especially if you're traveling in August or over Greek holidays. You can also buy tickets at the port on the day of travel, but you risk finding popular crossings sold out.

Arrive at the port at least 30 minutes before departure. Greek ferries don't wait for latecomers, and boarding closes 10 minutes before the scheduled time. Keep your ticket and ID handy for boarding.

Getting Around Kythnos

Once you dock at Merichas, you have several options for exploring the island. Local buses run between Merichas, messaria (the main town, also called Chora), and Loutra. Another route connects Merichas with Dryopida and Kanala. The buses coordinate with ferry arrivals during summer, making it easy to reach your accommodation without a car.

Taxis and Water Taxis

Taxis wait at the port when ferries arrive. If you're heading somewhere the bus doesn't serve, or you have heavy luggage, a taxi makes sense. Water taxis operate from Merichas to various beaches around the island, offering a scenic alternative to road travel when the sea is calm.

Renting Wheels

A rental car or scooter gives you freedom to explore at your own pace. Several agencies operate near the port in Merichas. Book ahead in August to guarantee availability. Keep in mind that some roads on Kythnos remain unpaved, especially those leading to remote beaches. A small car handles these better than a low-slung sedan, and a scooter works fine if you're comfortable on rough surfaces and don't mind dust.

Timing Your Arrival

Morning ferries let you arrive with a full day ahead to settle in and explore. Evening arrivals work if you've arranged accommodation pickup or know the bus schedule. During summer, the island stays lively after dark, so reaching Merichas at sunset doesn't mean missing out on dinner options.

The journey to Kythnos rewards you with an island that feels authentically Greek without the crowds of more famous Cyclades destinations. Whether you choose the quick hop from Lavrio or the longer sail from Piraeus, the trip itself becomes part of the experience.
