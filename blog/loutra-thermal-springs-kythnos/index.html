Loutra and the Thermal Springs of Kythnos

Written by Vrettos Goumas · 2026-06-30

Why Kythnos Was Once Called Thermia

The island had a different name for centuries. Thermia. The word comes directly from the thermal springs at Loutra, a small seaside village on the northeast coast where warm mineral water has bubbled up from the earth since antiquity.

I always tell guests who ask about unusual things to do on Kythnos to visit Loutra at least once. Not because it is dramatic or Instagram-perfect, but because sitting in naturally heated seawater while waves lap around you is an experience you will not find on many Greek islands.

Two Springs, one Beach

Loutra has two thermal springs. Kakavos and Agioi Anargyroi. Both send warm, iron-rich mineral water down to the sea.

The water does not stay in pools. It flows straight into the Aegean and mixes with the cold seawater right there on the beach. You can bathe in the warm stream for free, in the open air, any time you like.

The temperature varies depending on the tide and the season, but the water is always noticeably warmer than the surrounding sea. On cooler days in spring or autumn, the contrast is even more striking.

How to Find the Warm Water

Walk along the beach at Loutra. Look for places where the sand or pebbles are stained rust-orange from the iron in the water. That is where the warm streams meet the sea.

You will see other people sitting in the shallows there. The locals know the spots.

Some visitors bring small stones to build temporary walls in the sand, creating little pools where the warm water collects before mixing with the cold. This is not necessary, but it does let you control the temperature a bit if you want a proper soak.

We tell our guests to go early in the morning or late in the afternoon when the beach is quieter. Midday in summer can get crowded.

The Village and the Beach

Loutra is small. A handful of tavernas, a small marina, an organized beach with sunbeds and umbrellas if you want them.

The village has a calm, slightly old-fashioned feeling. It does not try to be anything other than what it is: a low-key seaside settlement that has drawn people to its healing waters for thousands of years.

I like eating lunch at one of the tavernas after a swim. The setting is simple, the food is straightforward Greek cooking, and you can sit in the shade and watch the boats in the little harbour.

The Historic Hydrotherapy Spa

A proper spa operated in Loutra during the 19th and 20th centuries. People came from Athens and beyond for hydrotherapy treatments, staying for weeks at a time to take the waters.

The building still stands, though it no longer functions as a spa in the way it once did. You can see it when you walk through the village. The architecture is modest but dignified, a reminder of the era when thermal springs were taken seriously as medicine, not just leisure.

The spa culture has faded, but the springs themselves have not changed. The same warm, mineral-laden water that drew visitors a century ago still flows into the sea today.

Getting to Loutra

Loutra is an easy drive from both Chora and the port of Merichas. The road is paved and well-maintained.

If you are staying with us and want to visit, I recommend going in the late afternoon. Swim in the warm water as the sun starts to drop, then stay for dinner at one of the tavernas. The drive back to your apartment in the evening is short and pleasant.

What to Bring

Bring a towel, water, and perhaps a hat if you are going during the day. The beach has some natural shade, but not much.

Wear old swimwear if you are particular about stains. The iron in the water can leave faint rust marks on fabric, though they usually wash out.

No special equipment is needed. This is not a spa with rules and fees. It is just a beach where the water happens to be warm.

A Quiet, honest Experience

Loutra will not overwhelm you. It will not make you gasp or reach for your camera every few minutes.

What it offers is simpler. Warm water. A quiet beach. A village that has not changed much in decades. The knowledge that you are sitting in the same spot where ancient Greeks once came to bathe, for exactly the same reason.

I think that is worth an afternoon.
