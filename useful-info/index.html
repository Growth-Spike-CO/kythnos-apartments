Useful Information for Your Kythnos Stay

Planning a trip to Kythnos means preparing for an island that moves at its own pace.

Getting Here

Ferries dock at Merichas, the main port on the west coast. This is where your island adventure begins, whether you're arriving from Piraeus or hopping over from a neighbouring Cycladic island.

Getting Around

The island has a small bus network connecting the main settlements. Buses run between Merichas, chora and Loutra, and there's also a service linking Merichas with Dryopida and Kanala. Timetables tend to match ferry arrivals during the summer months, but services are limited outside peak season.

Taxis are available, though not always abundant. Water taxis operate during summer, offering a scenic way to reach beaches that are awkward to access by land.

Many visitors rent a car or scooter, which gives you the freedom to explore at your own rhythm. Be prepared for some unpaved roads, especially when heading to more remote beaches and villages. A small car handles the terrain better than you might expect, and the distances are never overwhelming.

When to Visit

May through October offers the most reliable weather and the fullest range of services. July and August bring the warmest seas and the liveliest atmosphere, along with the highest prices and the most crowded beaches. June and September offer a sweet spot: warm enough for swimming, quiet enough to feel like you've discovered something special.

Outside these months, expect many businesses to close, and ferry schedules to thin out considerably.

Money

Bring cash. ATMs exist in Chora and Merichas, but they're not everywhere, and they can run dry during busy weekends. Many smaller tavernas, beach bars and family-run shops prefer cash or only accept cash. It's worth withdrawing more than you think you'll need when you spot a machine.

Card payments are becoming more common in larger establishments, but don't count on it universally.

Health and Emergencies

Chora has a pharmacy and basic medical facilities. For anything serious, you'll need to travel to Athens or another larger island. The EU-wide emergency number is 112.

Bring any prescription medications you need, along with basics like sunscreen, after-sun care and remedies for minor stomach upsets. Pharmacies stock common medications, but specific brands may not be available.

Practical Bits

Electricity runs at 230V, using plug types C and F (the standard two-pin European plugs). If you're coming from outside Europe, bring an adapter.

The island operates on Eastern European Time (EET), two hours ahead of GMT.

Greek is the local language, but English is widely understood in hotels, restaurants and shops that cater to visitors. Learning a few basic Greek phrases earns you warm smiles and sometimes better service. Even a simple "kalimera" (good morning) or "efcharisto" (thank you) goes a long way.
